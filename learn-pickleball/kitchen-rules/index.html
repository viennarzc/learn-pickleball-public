What is the kitchen in pickleball?

The kitchen is the non-volley zone beside the net. You can stand in it—you just cannot volley while touching it.

Rule baseline: GPF and USA Pickleball · Reviewed 1 Sep 2026

The short version

The non-volley zone, commonly called the kitchen, is the area immediately beside the net. It exists to stop players from standing at the net and hitting every ball out of the air.

Allowed

Stand in the kitchen, walk through it, or enter it to play a ball after it bounces.

Fault

Volley while touching the kitchen or its line. It is also a fault if your momentum after a volley carries you into it.

What does “volley” mean?

A volley is a ball you hit before it bounces on your side. That is the key distinction: a bounced ball can be played from the kitchen; a ball in the air cannot be volleyed from there.

Remember it: bounce first, step in; ball in the air, stay out.

Momentum still counts

Jumping from outside the kitchen does not automatically make a volley legal. If the shot’s momentum carries you into the non-volley zone after contact, it is a fault. Regain balance outside the zone before you volley again.
