# Pickleball Visual Curriculum and Coverage Manifest

Research synthesis reviewed 19 August 2026. This inventory distinguishes official rules from recommended fundamentals, coaching choices, and advanced plays.

## Research baseline

- The [2026 Global Pickleball Federation Rulebook](https://globalpickleballfederation.org/pickleball-rulebook/) and [2026 USA Pickleball rules](https://usapickleball.org/rules/) are the rule baseline.
- [USA Pickleball beginner fundamentals](https://usapickleball.org/pickleball-skills/level-one/pickleball-fundamentals-for-beginners/) and its [basic terms guide](https://usapickleball.org/pickleball-skills/level-one/pickleball-basic-terms/) are the primary technique baseline.
- USA Pickleball's [kitchen footwork](https://usapickleball.org/pickleball-skills/level-four/basic-kitchen-line-footwork-to-move-more-efficiently/), [advanced footwork](https://usapickleball.org/pickleball-skills/level-three/advanced-pickleball-footwork-crossover-and-drop-steps-at-the-kitchen-line/), [split-step](https://usapickleball.org/pickleball-skills/level-four/how-to-master-the-split-step-for-faster-reactions/), and [positioning](https://usapickleball.org/strategies/pickleball-basics-positioning-tips/) lessons inform movement assets.
- USA Pickleball's [health and safety hub](https://usapickleball.org/health-and-safety-hub/) and [Mayo Clinic injury-prevention advice](https://sportsmedicine.mayoclinic.org/news/mayo-clinic-minute-how-to-prevent-injuries-when-playing-pickleball/) inform attire and readiness assets.
- The [Pickleball.com shot library](https://pickleball.com/docs/en/article/pickleball-shots-techniques-library-every-shot-you-need-to-know) is a secondary coaching source for shot taxonomy, including advanced plays. These are not official requirements.

## Complete subject inventory

### 1. Equipment and setup

- Paddle anatomy, weight, edge guard, face, handle, and comfortable grip size.
- Indoor and outdoor perforated balls.
- Court shoes with lateral support; eye protection; breathable layers; hat, sunscreen, and water.
- Safe court check: dry surface, secure laces, clear bags and loose balls.
- Avoid running shoes for repeated lateral play, loose jewelry, obstructed vision, and apparel close to the ball color.

### 2. Paddle grips

- Continental / neutral beginner grip with the hand's V aligned along the paddle edge.
- Eastern forehand variation.
- Semi-western or western forehand variation as a specialized option.
- Relaxed-to-firm grip pressure and small adjustments between soft and fast exchanges.
- Two-handed backhand hand placement.
- Avoid thumb stacked above the index finger, a rigid “death grip,” choking onto the paddle face, unsupported wrist collapse, and mid-rally grip searching.

USA Pickleball recommends continental for beginners. Other grips are coaching options, not laws; published coaching materials vary in how they prioritize them.

### 3. Stances and posture

- Universal ready position: balanced athletic base, bent knees, chest quiet, paddle in front.
- Return-of-serve stance with space to move forward.
- Serve stance behind the baseline.
- Compact kitchen-line stance and low-ball lunge.
- Transition-zone split step timed near opponent contact.
- Side-on overhead preparation and recovery stance.
- Standing and sport-wheelchair ready-position variations.
- Avoid upright locked knees, feet too close, excessive forward bend at the waist, heels planted, paddle hanging at the thigh, and weight falling backward.

### 4. Footwork

- Side shuffle at the kitchen line.
- Split step and first-step reaction.
- Forward transition after a return or drop, followed by a balanced stop before the opponent hits.
- Backhand-side crossover for short wide balls.
- Defensive 45-degree drop step for deeper or faster balls.
- Turn-and-run or sideways shuffle for overheads.
- Recovery to a useful base position and partner movement as a linked unit.
- Avoid crossing over on routine forehand-side kitchen movement, backpedaling under a lob, running through contact, reaching instead of moving, remaining static after a shot, and crowding a partner.

### 5. Serve, return, and groundstrokes

- Volley serve and drop serve.
- Diagonal placement and legal contact context.
- Compact forehand and backhand groundstrokes.
- Deep return followed by movement toward the kitchen line.
- Third-shot drive and controlled fifth-shot continuation.
- Two-handed backhand option.
- Avoid oversized tennis-style backswing, wrist-only swing, late contact behind the body, open paddle face on a drive, and admiring the shot instead of recovering.

### 6. Soft game and volleys

- Forehand and backhand dink with contact in front and a quiet wrist.
- Cross-court dink and patient neutral dink.
- Third-shot drop with a gentle arc into the non-volley zone.
- Punch, roll, block, and drop volley.
- Transition-zone reset with a stable base and soft hands.
- Speed-up recognition and compact counter.
- Avoid scooping, taking a large backswing, snapping the wrist, overreaching into the kitchen, and attacking an unattackable ball from below net height.

### 7. Power and finishing shots

- Forehand drive, backhand drive, and topspin roll volley.
- Offensive lob versus defensive lob context.
- Overhead smash with side turn, high contact in front, and safe recovery.
- Backhand counter and compact flick or roll as advanced options.
- Avoid backing straight under a lob, contacting behind the head, swinging across a partner's space, and using power without recovery balance.

### 8. Advanced and trick-shot library

- Erne: volley beside the kitchen after legally establishing position outside the non-volley zone.
- Around-the-post (ATP): a ball traveling outside the net post into the opponent's court.
- Bert: an Erne taken on a partner's side as an advanced team play.
- Backhand flick/roll, scorpion counter, tomahawk-style counter, tweener, and behind-the-back improvisation.
- Poach, stack, and fake as team tactics rather than trick shots.

These are low-priority advanced coaching concepts. Their illustrations must include court context and should never imply that novelty is required for good play.

### 9. Court, rules, and decisions

- Baselines, sidelines, centerlines, service courts, kitchen, net, and out-of-bounds space.
- Serve destination, two-bounce rule, line status, volley momentum, kitchen contact, partner kitchen contact, and what is not a kitchen fault.
- Doubles score call, server number, side-out, and service rotation.
- Starting and recovery positions for singles, doubles, and serving/receiving teams.
- Ball-in/out decisions, replay situations, clear score calling, and fair line-call convention.

### 10. Partner play

- Move forward and laterally as a linked pair.
- Maintain playable spacing without leaving a large middle gap.
- Yield overheads and avoid paddle collisions through early communication.
- Cover middle by role and ball position rather than both players chasing.
- Stack and switch as advanced options.
- Avoid split levels, one player stranded in transition, both partners reaching for the same ball, and silent poaching.

### 11. Safety, attire, and readiness

- Dynamic warm-up, balance preparation, gradual practice volume, and cooldown.
- Court shoes, eye protection, secure laces, breathable clothing, hat/sunscreen, hydration, and suitable weather layers.
- Comfortable paddle grip size and manageable equipment weight.
- Sideways shuffle or turn-and-run instead of backpedaling for a lob.
- Avoid ball-colored clothing where it can interfere with visibility, wet courts, loose balls underfoot, and playing through pain.

Available injury research is largely observational and shows falls are common in emergency-department data, particularly among older players. These practical visuals communicate risk reduction, not a promise that injury can be prevented. See the [descriptive epidemiology study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11758564/) and [2024 review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39079099/).

### 12. Inclusive and adaptive play

- Sport-wheelchair ready position, chair recovery, and court movement.
- Standing/wheelchair doubles spacing.
- Two-bounce variation and service context only where the current rulebook specifies it.
- Hearing, vision, and mobility-friendly teaching cues: clear court contrast, captioned instructions, and non-audio-only signals.

## Editorial notes

- Use exact rule language only in written copy that links to the current rulebook; the image should show the situation.
- Teach a beginner default first, then reveal alternatives. Continental grip and a balanced ready position are starting points, not moral judgments.
- Do not use “wrong” for harmless personal variation. Reserve “fault” for rule outcomes and “avoid” for safety or a clearly limiting habit.
- Re-review rule-sensitive assets annually and advanced technique sources when their linked page changes.
